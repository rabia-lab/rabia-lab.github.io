NDSU Fargo, North Dakota

The Rabia Precision Agriculture Lab develops sensor-driven and AI-enabled solutions to advance precision agriculture, sustainability, and data-informed decision-making.

Our research integrates remote sensing, UAVs, robotics, spatial analytics, and machine learning to support adaptive, field-scale management of crops and soils.

Based at North Dakota State University, we work closely with farmers, industry partners, and interdisciplinary teams to translate research into practice.

Research areas

All areas →

  • Dynamic Precision Agriculture Management

    Remote sensing, drone scans, and AI working together to monitor crop growth, field conditions, and produce quality through the season — so management decisions can respond to what the field is actually doing.

  • Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) & Remote Sensing

    Capturing multispectral and RGB imagery of crop fields to detect stress early, measure plant traits, and give growers something concrete to act on.

  • BVLOS High-Altitude Operations

    Flying beyond visual line of sight at survey altitude to extend how much ground we can cover in a single flight, and testing what image quality at that range means for the remote sensing work it feeds.

  • Robotics & Autonomous Systems

    Field robotics and autonomous ground platforms for precision weed and crop management.

  • Laser Weeding

    Precise, non-chemical control of herbicide-resistant weeds, using a laser to destroy weeds where they stand without disturbing the soil or the surrounding crop.

  • Variable Rate Management

    No two parts of a field need the same treatment. We work out how much seed, fertilizer, and chemistry each area actually needs, so inputs go where they make a difference instead of being spread evenly and wasted.

  • Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning

    Deep learning and computer vision for high-throughput phenotyping, disease detection, and trait quantification.

  • Plant & Soil Phenotyping

    Quantifying crop and soil traits across spatial and temporal scales to support sustainable management.

  • Precision Livestock Farming (PLF)

    Remote sensing and automated GPS tracking to locate animals across an operation, follow their movement, and keep their records current without hands-on monitoring.
